How this works

Each measurement is divided by height in meters to give an index (mm/m), which is compared with the reference mean ± 2 SD. % of expected is the index divided by the reference mean; the z-score is (index − mean) ÷ SD. With these references the upper limit falls at roughly 122–126% of expected, depending on the segment. "Equivalent at this patient's height" is the limit (mm/m) multiplied by height (m).

Report text: where both references apply, tap the radio button next to NORRE or WASE to choose which one a card's statement uses (your last choice becomes the default for the next patient). The chips under each statement turn pieces of the sentence on or off for all cards, and are remembered. You can also click the statement to edit it before copying; a change to the measurement or settings rebuilds it.

NORRE provides values for both timings and both edge conventions (plus sinus-to-commissure dimensions at end-diastole, inner edge). WASE values are age-specific, were measured at end-diastole using the leading edge convention, and cover SOV and STJ only.
